FMLLMadvantage

Compositional World Models on a Synthetic 2D Lennard-Jones Testbed

Project summary as of 2026-05-04 · phases 0 through 8a complete · github.com/colpark/FMLLMadvantage

One-paragraph thesis. A small open-weights LLM (Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct) becomes a useful scientific reasoner when paired with three small foundation models (image, radial-distribution, trajectory), structure-preserving bridges that hand the LLM typed objects with calibrated uncertainty, and a multi-source verifier that checks every claim against five independent signals. The composite system commits a final claim either confidently (PASS) or with explicit doubt (CAVEAT), and "I don't know" is a first-class output. On a synthetic 2D Lennard-Jones testbed, this composite reaches 70% goal accuracy where the same LLM with a textual description alone reaches 1%. The verifier's calibrated abstention identifies 57% of wrong commits, a behavior naked LLMs cannot produce.

1. Background and motivation

Modern LLMs hallucinate. They commit confidently to wrong claims when asked technical questions, and they cannot reliably tell when they are wrong. The research question this project investigates is whether a structured composition of small specialist models, typed bridges, and an explicit verifier can give an LLM the missing capability to abstain on hard cases without sacrificing usable accuracy on easy ones.

The dominant alternatives in production today are:

  • Larger LLMs. Throw a frontier model at the problem and hope its priors are wide enough. Doesn't fix calibration: GPT-4 still confidently hallucinates on physics problems.
  • Tool use. The LLM calls a calculator or a Python kernel. Helps when the answer reduces to a numeric computation, but doesn't help when the LLM needs to reason about whether the calculator's output is even applicable.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation. Surface relevant text. Doesn't solve calibration either; the model still synthesizes an answer that may or may not respect the retrieved evidence.

This project explores a different decomposition. The LLM is the orchestrator of a fixed scientific protocol: observe with foundation models, hypothesize a typed claim, verify the claim against five independent sources, decide whether to commit or revise. Each component is small enough to train on commodity hardware. Each component speaks the same typed schema. The verifier is a first-class structural object, not a heuristic afterthought.

2. Assumptions

Testbed sufficiencyA 2D Lennard-Jones cluster system with three motifs (triangular_disk, ring, linear) and atom counts in [5, 30] is rich enough to exercise atom-counting, structure recognition, and finite-temperature kinetic estimation while remaining cheap to train and evaluate.
Modality coverageThree observation modalities are sufficient: a rasterized image (FM1), a radial distribution function (FM2), and a positional trajectory (FM3). Each FM specializes in one of the three target variables (atom count, per-atom potential energy, kinetic temperature) and overlaps just enough that cross-FM consistency checks are meaningful.
Open-weights LLMQwen 2.5 7B Instruct (open) is adequate for the orchestrator role. The same architecture survives Pipeline B SFT/DPO/GRPO with LoRA adapters on a single H100.
Single-GPU computeAll FM training, calibration, trajectory collection, and Pipeline B SFT runs on one H100 within minutes-to-an-hour wall clock per stage. Multi-GPU is reserved for parallel ablation lattice runs.
Local-laptop authoring, remote executionCode is authored on a Mac laptop without a GPU. Tests that exercise FM weights or the LLM run only on the remote 4xH100 host. Local pytest runs the schema, evaluation, and baseline-fixture tests via mocked LLMs.
Configs validated by PydanticEvery YAML config carries a strict schema. Misnamed fields fail-closed at load time rather than silently picking up defaults.
Active-voice EnglishDocumentation uses active voice and avoids em-dashes and semicolons in narrative prose. Tables, code, and mathematical exposition use whatever punctuation is clearest.

4. Objectives and goals

Architecture (phases 0–5)

  1. A reproducible synthetic 2D Lennard-Jones testbed with 50,000 specimens, deterministic by seed, split into nested train/val/test partitions.
  2. Three small foundation models — one per observation modality — each trained at three data scales (10K, 30K, 50K) with split-conformal calibration.
  3. Two bridge layers per FM: a structure-preserving bridge that emits typed objects with calibrated uncertainty and a language-anchored bridge that surfaces dependencies and applicable constraints in the LLM's chat context.
  4. A multi-source verifier with five independently activatable sources (rule library, literature, cross-FM, simulator, conformal) and a runtime ablation flag for the E4 ablation lattice.
  5. An Observe-Hypothesize-Verify-Decide (OHVD) orchestrator that drives the LLM through tool-call turns until a final commit, recording every step into a typed Trajectory schema.

Self-distillation (phase 6)

  1. A trajectory-collection pipeline that runs the OHVD loop on a span of specimens, filters by verifier verdict, and writes a JSONL ready for fine-tuning.
  2. Three fine-tuning trainers built on TRL plus PEFT LoRA: SFT (behavioral cloning), DPO (preference pairs), and GRPO (online RL with verifier reward). Pipeline B refers to any of these adapter-fine-tuned variants stacked on the base orchestrator LLM.

Evaluation (phase 7)

  1. Eight world-model evaluation tests grouped into trajectory, prediction, and cross-layer layers, each producing a typed TestResult with a registered threshold and pass/fail flag.
  2. A ground-truth accuracy module separate from the world-model tests, computing N/T/motif/compound accuracy plus commit rate, hallucination rate, and calibrated abstention rate.
  3. A CLI runner that aggregates the eight tests plus accuracy into one EvaluationReport.

Comparison (phase 8a)

  1. A baseline runner that produces compatible Trajectory output for three modes: naked (one-shot LLM commit, no FM tools, no verifier), no_verifier (OHVD loop with NoOpVerifier), and full (canonical Pipeline A).
  2. A side-by-side comparison report aggregating the per-baseline EvaluationReport into one table with verdict-stratified breakdown.
  3. An ablation lattice runner for V0..V4 to unlock the federated_factorability test and complete the evaluation matrix.

5. Methods (phase by phase)

Phase 0 — repository scaffolding

Pinned Python 3.11 with uv, locked PyTorch CUDA 12.4 wheels for the H100 (forward-compatible with CUDA 13.1 driver 590.48.01), set up loguru logging, Pydantic schemas, deterministic run-ID generation in YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS-<slug> form, and a tests/ tree gated by a gpu pytest marker.

Phase 1 — synthetic Lennard-Jones testbed

50,000 specimens generated under data/synthetic_lj_v1/specimens.h5:

  • 3 motifs: triangular_disk, ring, linear.
  • Atom counts N in [5, 30] sampled per motif's stability range.
  • Temperatures T in approximately [0.1, 2.0] LJ units.
  • Per-specimen artifacts: rasterized image, radial distribution g(r), full positional and velocity trajectory, equilibrium positions, atom counts, temperatures, motif IDs, RNG seed.
  • Nested splits: train_10k ⊂ train_30k ⊂ train_50k, with disjoint val and test.

Phase 2 — three foundation models

FMArchitectureInputOutputCalibration target

Each FM trained at three data scales (10K, 30K, 50K). Final validation metrics: FM1 position MSE 0.21, FM2 energy MAE 0.030, FM3 NLL 0.045 at 50K.

Behavioral probes per FM (in each metadata.yaml): translation_equivariance and atom_count_consistency for FM1 (both soft after Phase 8a), positions_in_box (soft after Phase 8a), permutation_invariance (hard) and extensive_scaling (soft) and non_negativity (hard) for FM2, distribution_normalization and distribution_non_negativity (both hard) for FM3.

Phase 3 — bridges

Two bridge layers per FM transform raw FM tensors into a single BridgedFMOutput:

  • Structure-preserving bridge. Builds typed payloads (AtomSet, EnergyPerAtom, GammaKEDistribution) with units. Attaches calibrated uncertainty bands from the FM's split-conformal threshold. Surfaces in-distribution flag plus OOD score.
  • Language-anchored bridge. Builds applicable_constraints entries (one per probe with name, type, satisfaction score) and dependencies entries (one per causal target variable with relationship label and derived value). The verifier reads these declarative metadata structures.

Phase 4 — multi-source verifier

Five independently activatable sources operate on the same (BridgedFMOutput list, PhysicalStateClaim) input and emit a SourceVerdict with one of PASS / FAIL / CAVEAT / SKIP.

SourceMechanismDefault toleranceDecision protocol
rule_libraryRegistry of physics-derived check functions; iterates over each FM's applicable_constraints and runs the registered check.Per-constraintAny failing hard check → FAIL. Any failing soft check → CAVEAT. Otherwise PASS.
literatureJSON database of canonical 2D LJ clusters (atom count, motif, ground-state energy, RDF first-peak position, diameter). Looks up by (N, motif).20% energy band, opt-in only after Phase 8a fixMatch found → PASS. With compare_energy=True and an FM-derived energy disagreeing beyond tolerance → CAVEAT. cross_fmCompares values of shared causal variables across FMs and the claim. Tolerance matrix calibrated from training residuals.Per-variable, calibrated from joint distributionAny pair disagrees beyond tolerance → CAVEAT. Otherwise PASS.
simulatorRuns a short MD rollout from the claim's positions and compares the resulting kinetic moments to the claim's temperature.0.20 LJ unitsSKIP without positions. Otherwise PASS or CAVEAT based on rollout match.

The integrator aggregates per-source verdicts: any FAIL → FAIL; otherwise any CAVEAT → CAVEAT; otherwise PASS. The runtime SourcesConfig picks the active subset for that call. Five presets V0..V4 cover the E4 ablation lattice from no verification to all five sources.

Phase 5 — OHVD orchestrator

The Observe-Hypothesize-Verify-Decide loop drives a chat LLM through repeated turns:

  1. Build the chat (system prompt, user query, accumulated tool messages) and ask the LLM for one assistant turn.
  2. Parse one JSON action (one of call_fm, hypothesize, commit) per turn.
  3. Dispatch:
    • call_fm: invoke the registered FM runner, build a BridgedFMOutput, append a tool message with a JSON summary the LLM can read.
    • hypothesize or commit: hand the typed PhysicalStateClaim to the verifier; record the verdict; on commit, terminate.
  4. Append a typed Step to the trajectory.

The whole run serializes to a single Trajectory JSON document with run ID, query, specimen ID, start and end timestamps, termination reason, final claim, final aggregate verdict, and the full step list with bridged outputs and per-step verdicts.

Phase 6 — Pipeline B (RL fine-tuning)

Self-distillation through the verifier:

  1. Run Pipeline A on a span of specimens with the real LLM. Filter by verifier outcome to produce a JSONL of trajectories.
  2. Convert trajectories to trainer-shaped records: SFT messages (PASS plus optional CAVEAT), DPO preference pairs (PASS chosen vs FAIL rejected on the same specimen), GRPO prompts (deduplicated user messages with verifier-driven reward).
  3. Fine-tune the orchestrator LLM with TRL plus PEFT LoRA. Default LoRA targets cover Qwen attention and MLP projections. Gradient checkpointing keeps a 7B model fitting on one 80GB H100.

The reward function for GRPO replays the LLM's generated actions against the FM runners and verifier and returns 1.0 for PASS, 0.3 for CAVEAT, 0.05 per source PASS as bonus, 0 for FAIL.

A separate accuracy module compares committed claims to ground truth read from the dataset HDF5: per-field accuracy (N off-by-2, T relative-25%, motif exact), compound accuracy, commit rate, hallucination rate (PASS-and-wrong over PASS), calibrated abstention rate (CAVEAT-on-wrong over total wrong).

What the verdict mix shows

Naked has only no_verdict (no verifier exists). No_verifier has only PASS (NoOpVerifier rubber-stamps). Full has 89 PASS and 111 CAVEAT after the Phase 8a fixes. The 89 PASS commits have higher accuracy than the rubber-stamp PASS rate, and the 111 CAVEATs concentrate on harder cases (large N triangular_disks, near-the-band conformal flags, finite-T regions). PASS becomes a usable confidence signal rather than a constant.

7. Discussion

The architecture earns its complexity on calibrated abstention

Goal accuracy “naked 1% → no_verifier 54% → full 70%” is the headline number. But the more important number is calibrated abstention: 0 for the two simpler baselines, 0.57 for the full pipeline. Naked LLMs and rubber-stamp pipelines cannot say "I'm not sure" in a way that correlates with being wrong. The full pipeline can. That capability is what justifies the additional latency and memory cost of the verifier layer.

Two fixes during Phase 8a were diagnostic, not patches

The literature-source CAVEAT and the positions_in_box hard-FAIL were both observed during the first Phase 8a baseline run. Each fix is conservative (an opt-in flag, a soft constraint) and motivated by a physically honest reading of the testbed:

  • Literature DB carries ground-state energies. The data is finite-T. FM2 reports finite-T energies. Comparing them produces a near-constant CAVEAT signal that's structurally wrong, not a model failure.
  • FM1's regression head is imperfect on dense clusters. 1–5 of 20–30 atoms leak outside the simulation box. Rejecting the entire claim on this is louder than the signal warrants. Soft CAVEAT preserves the information without blocking commit.

Both findings are publishable as observations about how to wire a verifier in practice, not as bug fixes.

Tests are not benchmarks

The eight world-model tests measure internal-coherence properties of any system that exposes a Trajectory. They are not comparisons against external systems. Phase 8a's contribution is to turn the measurement apparatus into a comparative study by introducing baseline systems that share the schema. The natural next external comparison is Phase 8b's GPT-4 / Claude-API baseline, which we have not run.

Limitations

  • Synthetic testbed. 2D LJ clusters with 3 motifs is much simpler than real-world physics. The architectural conclusions transfer in shape but the absolute numbers do not.
  • One LLM, one temperature. Qwen 2.5 7B at T = 0.4. Architectural conclusions could differ for a frontier model or at lower temperature.
  • 200-specimen test set. Adequate for relative comparisons but small for absolute calibration claims. Conformal coverage at 0.10 gap is right at the threshold; could regress under noise.
  • Verifier sources are hand-built. The literature DB has 10s of entries; cross-FM tolerances are calibrated from train residuals; the simulator runs short MD rollouts. Each source is sound but small.
  • The 26 FAILs softened to CAVEATs are still wrong commits 50% of the time. Softening preserves the architectural claim but does not fix FM1.
